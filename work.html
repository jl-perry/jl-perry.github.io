Selected work

Most of my published work is applied: prediction problems in medicine, done with clinicians at the University of Washington. My doctoral research is methodological, on finding direct relationships among many variables. Collaborators have included the UW CLEAR Center, the Cardiovascular Health Research Unit, the Cancer Vaccine Institute, and pediatric neurologists at UCSF.

No. 01 2024 Circulation

Predicting out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in the general population

First author · with collaborators at the UW Cardiovascular Health Research Unit

Most out-of-hospital cardiac arrests happen in people who weren’t surfaced by current clinical risk pathways. I led a study using electronic health record data to identify at-risk individuals across the general population, improving on existing approaches and surfacing non-medical predictors (substance use disorder, fluid and electrolyte imbalances, marital status, demographic factors) that conventional screens don’t capture.

Subject-level spinal osteoporotic fracture prediction

Co-first author · with collaborators at the UW CLEAR Center

No. 03 2023 Biometrics

Selective prediction-set models with coverage rate guarantees

With Jean Feng, Anand Sondhi, and Noah Simon

Selective prediction-set models return either a calibrated prediction set or an abstention. This work develops methods that produce such sets with provable coverage, including under the model’s own selective behavior.

No. 04 2017 CSE 546, UW Best in class · 150+ students

Phytaid — phytolith image classification

Final project for CSE 546 (Machine Learning) · led to a grant and an RA position

An automatic classifier for grass phytoliths — the silica microstructures left behind in soil that paleobotanists, archaeologists, and anthropologists use to reconstruct past plant communities. The discriminative side: multi-style deep features on top of ResNet-18 with metric learning, reaching 96.7% accuracy at subfamily and 97.0% at tribe level, 80.3% at genus. The generative side: conditional GAN with Wasserstein loss and gradient penalty for data augmentation. Won the Best Project Award in CSE 546 — UW’s largest graduate ML course — and the work continued as a funded research collaboration with Prof. Caroline Strömberg’s lab.

No. 01 2020 PhD coursework, UW

A framework for feature selection in clustering

Independent reproduction of Witten and Tibshirani (2010) · mentor: Daniela M. Witten

Reproduction of the sparse-clustering framework, end-to-end: the K-means and hierarchical objectives, the L1/L2 penalty geometry that drives sparsity, the algorithms, and the breast cancer and SNP experiments from the original paper. The comparison table at the end — the framework against three earlier methods across feature support, large-p feasibility, and clustering-method coverage — is the slide multiple people have asked to reproduce.

No. 03 2018 UW class project

Diagnosing Parkinson’s disease from audio data using deep neural networks and support vector machines

Sole author · mPower clinical study data (Sage Bionetworks)

A class paper applying machine learning to voice biomarkers for Parkinson’s diagnosis. Audio recordings from the mPower study — an iPhone-based clinical observational study — preprocessed with voice-activation detection, then run through both support vector machines and deep neural networks for binary classification of patients versus controls. The work explores how audio features can serve as cheap, accessible diagnostic signals for neurodegenerative disease, with hyperparameter tuning over the SVM regularization constant and DNN architecture choices.

No. 04 2017 Smith College RA Smith presentation · JMM

Portfolio performance modeling with PCA and backward elimination

With Eva Gjekmarkaj · supervisor: Professor Katherine Halvorsen · data from Green Square Capital

A statistical study of the thirteen financial factors Green Square Capital uses to evaluate company performance, applied to thirty-three traded companies across eight sectors plus sixty-two untraded. Two parallel approaches: backward elimination to drop non-significant predictors, and principal component analysis to reduce dimensionality while keeping all thirteen. The biplot at the end — loadings of EBITDA margin, EPS, DPS over five years, PE, and the rest, with companies projected into PC1×PC2 space — was the visualization the management team got the most out of. Presented at Smith College and at the Joint Mathematics Meeting in January 2017.
