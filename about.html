About

I’m a PhD candidate at the University of Amsterdam, where I expect to defend in 2027. I’m supervised by Jeroen de Mast and Reza Mohammadi at the University of Amsterdam and by Ali Shojaie at the University of Washington. My doctoral research develops a novel Bayesian approach to estimating sparse dependence structure in high-dimensional data, one that applies well beyond the Gaussian setting, to models where standard Bayesian methods are difficult to apply.

I’m naturally curious and an avid reader, and I have loved mathematics for as long as I can remember. The thread running through my education and research has been pursuing questions about the world around me. At St. John’s College, the curriculum is built on original texts: discussion-based seminars in philosophy, literature, and history, and four years of mathematics and science read from the sources, from Euclid’s Elements and Apollonius’s Conics to Newton’s Principia and Einstein.

I spent four years in planning at Abercrombie & Fitch, overseeing margin, including the pricing and promotions teams, for the international and flagship stores, abercrombie kids, and Abercrombie Men’s. Fashion is one of my passions, which is what drew me to the industry.

I returned to mathematics through Smith College’s NSF-funded post-baccalaureate program for women in mathematics, where I began teaching and doing research, and then earned an MS in statistics at the University of Washington on the Machine Learning and Big Data track. During the MS I spent a summer at Amazon Web Services, building a customer-churn model that went into production. I stayed on at UW as a research scientist in biostatistics, leading and contributing to research with faculty across statistics and medicine, mentoring graduate and undergraduate students, and publishing across statistics, cardiology, and radiology journals, including first-author work in Circulation.

Outside work I play the piano, read widely, and go to the opera and ballet. I also ski, strength train, and practice yoga and Pilates.

If any of this overlaps with your own work, I’d be glad to hear from you at j.l.perry@uva.nl.
